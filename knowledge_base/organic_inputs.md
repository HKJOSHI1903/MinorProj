# Organic and low-cost inputs

Source to verify against: National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF) and your Krishi Vigyan Kendra. Recipes vary by region; follow the proportions taught by your local KVK.

## Neem seed kernel extract

Neem seed kernel extract is a spray made from crushed neem seed kernels soaked overnight in water and filtered. It discourages sucking pests such as aphids, whiteflies and jassids and small caterpillars, and is gentle on helpful insects. A little soap is added so the spray sticks to the leaves. Spray in the evening and wet the underside of the leaves. It works best when pest numbers are still low and needs repeating after rain.

## Neem oil

Neem oil mixed with water and a little soap is used against sucking pests, mites and powdery mildew. Shake well, spray in the evening, and test on a few plants first because strong mixtures can burn tender leaves.

## Jeevamrut

Jeevamrut is a fermented liquid made from cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, a handful of soil and water. It is applied to the soil with irrigation water or sprayed in diluted form to increase helpful soil microbes. It is a soil conditioner, not a pesticide.

## Bordeaux mixture

Bordeaux mixture is a traditional fungicide made from copper sulphate and lime in water. It protects against blights, leaf spots and downy mildew. It must be prepared fresh, with the two solutions made separately in non-metal containers and then combined. Too much copper can burn leaves, so follow the proportions recommended by your KVK.

## Trichoderma

Trichoderma is a helpful fungus sold as a powder. It is mixed with seed before sowing or with farmyard manure and applied to the soil to protect roots from wilt and root rot fungi. Do not mix it with chemical fungicides.

## Compost and vermicompost

Compost is made by rotting crop residue, weeds and dung in a heap or pit kept moist and turned every few weeks. Vermicompost is made by earthworms from partly rotted organic waste and is richer in nutrients. Both improve soil structure, water holding and soil life.

## Mulching

Covering the soil around plants with dry leaves, straw or crop residue keeps moisture in, keeps weeds down and stops soil with disease spores from splashing onto the lower leaves.
