# Soil health and the Soil Health Card

Source to verify against: Soil Health Card portal of the Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare (soilhealth.dac.gov.in); your district soil testing laboratory.

## What the Soil Health Card is

The Soil Health Card is a printed report given to farmers under a Government of India scheme. A soil sample from your field is tested in a laboratory and the card shows the status of the soil and recommends how much fertiliser and manure to apply for different crops. Using it helps you avoid spending on fertiliser the soil does not need.

## What the card reports

The card reports major nutrients: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K). It reports sulphur, and micronutrients such as zinc, iron, copper, manganese and boron. It also reports soil pH (how acidic or alkaline the soil is), electrical conductivity (salt level) and organic carbon (a measure of organic matter). Each value is rated, usually as low, medium or high, with a fertiliser recommendation.

## How to take a soil sample

Take the sample after harvest and before applying fertiliser or manure. Do not sample near bunds, trees, manure heaps or wet spots. In each field, dig a V-shaped cut about 15 cm deep at 8 to 10 spots spread across the field in a zigzag, and take a thin slice of soil from each. Mix all the slices on a clean cloth, remove stones and roots, and keep about half a kilogram. Dry it in the shade, put it in a clean cloth or plastic bag with your name, village, survey number and the crop you plan to grow, and give it to the soil testing laboratory, agriculture office or Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Understanding soil pH

A pH of about 6.5 to 7.5 suits most crops. Below 6 the soil is acidic; liming materials are used to correct it. Above 8.5 the soil is alkaline; gypsum and organic matter are commonly used. Do not apply lime or gypsum without a soil test recommendation.

## What N, P and K do for the plant

Nitrogen gives green, leafy growth; a shortage shows as pale yellow older leaves and stunted plants. Phosphorus builds roots and helps flowering and seed formation; a shortage shows as dark or purplish leaves and poor root growth. Potassium strengthens the plant against disease, drought and lodging and improves grain and fruit quality; a shortage shows as yellowing and drying along the edges of older leaves.

## Improving soil health

Add farmyard manure, compost or vermicompost every year. Grow a green manure crop such as dhaincha or sunn hemp and plough it in before flowering. Include a pulse crop in the rotation, because pulses fix nitrogen from the air. Return crop residue to the soil instead of burning it; burning destroys organic matter and helpful soil life. Use biofertilisers such as Rhizobium for pulses and Azotobacter or phosphate solubilising bacteria for other crops. Apply chemical fertiliser in the amount the soil test recommends, in split doses.
