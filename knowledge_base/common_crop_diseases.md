# Common crop diseases and how to manage them

Source to verify against: ICAR crop protection advisories and your Krishi Vigyan Kendra. This is general guidance without doses; read the product label for the dose and waiting period.

## Late blight of potato and tomato

Late blight is caused by a water mould and spreads very fast in cool, cloudy, wet weather; it can destroy a field within days. It shows as pale green water-soaked patches on leaves that turn dark brown or black, often with white growth on the underside in humid weather. Act the same day you see it. Remove and destroy infected plants. Organic option: Bordeaux mixture or another copper product. Chemical option: mancozeb or chlorothalonil as a protective spray, or metalaxyl with mancozeb once the disease has appeared. Prevent it with certified seed, wide spacing, earthing up potato rows, and watering at the base in the morning. Do not grow tomato next to potato.

## Early blight of potato and tomato

Early blight is a fungal disease of older or stressed plants in warm weather. It shows as brown spots with rings like a target on the lower leaves, which then turn yellow and die. Remove infected lower leaves, mulch the soil and feed the crop well. Organic option: a copper product or a Trichoderma-based bio-fungicide. Chemical option: mancozeb or chlorothalonil at the first spots, repeated every 7 to 10 days.

## Powdery mildew

Powdery mildew is a fungal disease that looks like white powder on leaves and stems. It likes warm dry days with humid nights. Organic option: wettable sulphur, potassium bicarbonate or neem oil at the first sign. Space plants for air movement and avoid too much nitrogen.

## Bacterial spot of tomato and chilli

Bacterial spot shows as small dark greasy-looking spots on leaves and raised scabs on fruit. It arrives on infected seed or seedlings and spreads by rain splash and by handling wet plants. Use clean seed, rotate crops for two to three years, use drip irrigation and do not work in the crop when it is wet. Copper sprays slow it down.

## Leaf curl and mosaic viruses

Leaf curl virus is spread by whiteflies; mosaic virus spreads by contact with hands, tools and infected seed. Leaves curl, yellow or show a light and dark green pattern and plants stay stunted. There is no cure for a plant that has a virus. Pull out infected plants and destroy them. Control whiteflies with yellow sticky traps and neem oil, raise seedlings under insect-proof net, and wash hands and tools.

## Rust diseases

Rusts show as small raised reddish-brown or orange pustules that rub off like powder. They spread by wind in cool, moist weather. Grow resistant varieties and sow on time. A fungicide such as propiconazole or mancozeb is used only when the infection is heavy early in the season.

## Spider mites

Spider mites are tiny pests, not a disease. Leaves show fine yellow speckles, turn bronze and have fine webbing underneath. They multiply in hot, dry, dusty weather. Wash the underside of leaves with a strong jet of water, spray neem oil, and keep the crop well watered. Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides, which kill the natural enemies of mites.

## Why the same spray does not work on every disease

Fungicides work on fungal diseases, copper products help against bacterial diseases, and nothing cures a viral disease. That is why the disease must be identified before buying a product.
