# Safe use of pesticides

Source to verify against: product label and leaflet; Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee (CIBRC); your Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Before you buy

Identify the pest or disease first. Spraying the wrong product wastes money and can harm the crop. Buy only from a licensed dealer and ask for a bill. Check that the pack is sealed, has a label in a language you can read, and has not passed its expiry date. Buy only the amount needed for this season.

## Reading the label

The label and leaflet tell you the crop and pest the product is approved for, the dose per acre or per litre of water, how many days to wait between the last spray and harvest (the waiting period or pre-harvest interval), and first aid. The coloured triangle on the label shows how poisonous the product is: red is extremely toxic, yellow is highly toxic, blue is moderately toxic and green is slightly toxic. Prefer blue or green label products when they are effective.

## Dose and mixing

Use exactly the dose written on the label. A higher dose does not work better; it burns the crop, leaves more residue on the produce and makes pests resistant. Measure with a measuring cap, not by guess. Mix in a bucket with a stick, never with bare hands. Do not mix two products together unless the label or an agriculture officer says they can be mixed.

## Protecting yourself while spraying

Wear full sleeves, long trousers, shoes, gloves, a mask or cloth over the nose and mouth, and eye protection. Do not eat, drink, smoke or chew tobacco while spraying. Spray with the wind at your back so the mist blows away from you. Do not blow into a blocked nozzle with your mouth; clean it with a thin wire or water. Keep children and animals away from the field.

## When to spray

Spray in the early morning or late afternoon, when the air is calm and cool. Do not spray in strong wind, in the hot midday sun, or when rain is expected in the next few hours, because rain washes the product off before it works. Avoid spraying when the crop is flowering and bees are active; if you must, spray in the evening after bees have returned to the hive.

## After spraying

Wash the sprayer away from wells, ponds and canals. Wash your hands, face and body with soap, and wash the clothes you wore separately. Put up a sign or tell family and workers not to enter the field for the time stated on the label. Do not harvest or sell the crop before the waiting period on the label has passed.

## Storage and disposal

Keep pesticides in their original containers, locked, away from food, animal feed and children. Never store them in drinking water bottles or food containers. Rinse empty containers three times, pour the rinse water into the spray tank, then puncture and crush the containers so they cannot be reused. Do not burn them or throw them into water.

## If someone is poisoned

Signs include headache, dizziness, vomiting, heavy sweating, blurred vision, stomach pain and difficulty breathing. Move the person to fresh air, remove contaminated clothes and wash the skin with plenty of soap and water. If the product went into the eyes, wash them with clean water for 15 minutes. Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious person. Take the person to a doctor immediately and carry the pesticide container or label with you.
