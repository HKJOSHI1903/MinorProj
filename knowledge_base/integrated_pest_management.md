# Integrated pest and disease management

Source to verify against: ICAR and National Centre for Integrated Pest Management (NCIPM) crop-wise IPM packages; your Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## What integrated pest management means

Integrated pest management (IPM) means using several methods together to keep pests and diseases below the level that causes real loss, and using chemical pesticides only when they are needed. It lowers cost, protects the natural enemies of pests and slows down resistance.

## Prevention through good farming practice

Use certified, disease-free seed and seedlings of varieties that resist the common diseases of your area. Treat seed before sowing. Rotate crops: do not grow the same crop, or crops of the same family such as tomato, potato, brinjal and chilli, on the same land season after season. Plough deep in summer to expose soil insects and disease spores to the sun. Keep the right spacing so air moves between plants and leaves dry quickly. Remove weeds, which shelter pests and diseases. Remove and destroy infected plants, fallen leaves and crop residue instead of leaving them in the field.

## Watering and feeding

Too much nitrogen fertiliser makes soft, lush growth that attracts sucking pests and fungal diseases; apply fertiliser according to a soil test. Water at the base of the plant rather than over the leaves, and water in the morning so leaves are dry by night. Drip irrigation keeps leaves dry and reduces many leaf diseases. Avoid waterlogging, which causes root rot.

## Scouting the field

Walk through the field at least once a week and look at the underside of leaves, the growing tips and the stem base. Finding a problem early, when only a few plants are affected, makes it much easier to control. Remove the first infected leaves or plants by hand.

## Mechanical and biological methods

Yellow sticky traps catch whiteflies and aphids; blue sticky traps catch thrips. Pheromone traps attract and catch the moths of fruit borers and other caterpillars. Light traps catch many night-flying insects. Bird perches in the field help birds eat caterpillars. Protect helpful insects such as ladybird beetles, lacewings and spiders. Biological products include Trichoderma for soil and seed treatment against fungal diseases, Pseudomonas fluorescens against some bacterial and fungal diseases, and Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) against caterpillars.

## Botanical and home-made sprays

Neem seed kernel extract and neem oil sprays reduce sucking pests and small caterpillars and are safe for most helpful insects. Spray in the evening and cover the underside of the leaves. These sprays work best when pest numbers are still low.

## Chemical control as the last step

Use a chemical pesticide only when the pest or disease has crossed the level where it causes economic loss. Choose a product approved for that crop and problem, use the label dose, and do not repeat the same chemical group again and again; rotating groups slows resistance. Follow the waiting period before harvest.

## Fungal, bacterial and viral diseases are treated differently

Fungal diseases such as blights, leaf spots, rusts and mildews can be controlled with fungicides if treated early. Bacterial diseases such as bacterial spot are managed mainly with clean seed, copper sprays and keeping leaves dry. Viral diseases such as leaf curl and mosaic have no cure: remove infected plants and control the insects that spread the virus, such as whiteflies and aphids.
